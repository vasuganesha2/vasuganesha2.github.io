The Bound
If a connected graph \(G\) on \(n\ge 2\) vertices has no cut vertices (so it is 2-connected when \(n\ge 3\), with \(K_2\) also allowed), then \[ \boxed{\operatorname{diam}(G)\le \left\lfloor\frac n2\right\rfloor.} \]
A cut vertex is a vertex whose deletion disconnects the graph. For \(n\ge 3\), a connected graph with no cut vertices is 2-connected. The graph \(K_2\) is handled separately because it has only two vertices.
Proof by Contradiction
  1. Handle the small cases. If \(n=2\), then \(G=K_2\), so \(\operatorname{diam}(G)=1=\lfloor 2/2\rfloor\). For \(n\ge 3\), the graph is 2-connected because deleting any one vertex leaves it connected.
  2. Assume the opposite of the claim. For contradiction, suppose \(d>\lfloor n/2\rfloor\), where \(d=\operatorname{diam}(G)\). We will show that the vertex count forces \(n\ge 2d\), contradicting this assumption.
  3. Choose diametrically opposite vertices. Choose \(u,v\in V(G)\) such that their distance is the diameter: \[\operatorname{dist}(u,v)=d=\operatorname{diam}(G).\]
  4. Apply Menger's theorem. Since \(G\) is 2-connected, there exist two internally vertex-disjoint paths \(P_1\) and \(P_2\) from \(u\) to \(v\). They may share the endpoints \(u,v\), but they share no internal vertex.
  5. Lower-bound the number of internal vertices. By the definition of distance, every \(u\)-to-\(v\) path has length at least \(d\). Therefore each of \(P_1\) and \(P_2\) has at least \(d-1\) internal vertices: \[|V(P_1)\setminus\{u,v\}|\ge d-1,\qquad |V(P_2)\setminus\{u,v\}|\ge d-1.\]
  6. Count the distinct vertices. The two sets of internal vertices are disjoint, and neither contains \(u\) or \(v\). Hence \[\begin{aligned} n&\ge 2+(d-1)+(d-1)\\ &=2d. \end{aligned}\]
  7. Obtain the contradiction. The count gives \(n\ge 2d\), hence \(d\le n/2\). Since \(d\) is an integer, this means \(d\le\lfloor n/2\rfloor\), contradicting our assumption. Therefore the claimed bound holds: \[\boxed{d\le\left\lfloor\frac n2\right\rfloor.}\quad\square\]
Key Counting Idea
\[ \underbrace{2}_{\text{endpoints }u,v} +\underbrace{(d-1)}_{\text{internal vertices of }P_1} +\underbrace{(d-1)}_{\text{internal vertices of }P_2} \le n \] \[ \Longrightarrow\quad 2d\le n \quad\Longrightarrow\quad d\le\left\lfloor\frac n2\right\rfloor. \]

The crucial point is that the two paths have disjoint internal vertices. Without that disjointness, their internal vertices could overlap and the vertex count would not give the same bound.

Tightness: The Cycle Graph
\[ \operatorname{diam}(C_n)=\left\lfloor\frac n2\right\rfloor \qquad(n\ge 3). \]

Every cycle \(C_n\) has no cut vertices: deleting any one vertex leaves a path, which is connected. For two vertices chosen as far apart as possible, the shorter of the two arcs has length exactly \(\lfloor n/2\rfloor\). Thus the bound is tight.

Takeaway. The diameter is bounded because two internally vertex-disjoint routes between a farthest pair must each be at least as long as a shortest route. Their internal vertices cannot overlap, so the graph needs at least \(2d\) vertices.
